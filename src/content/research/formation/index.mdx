## Definition

**Formation** refers to the legal process of establishing a Limited Liability Company (LLC) under US state law. This involves filing formation documents with the Secretary of State and satisfying state-specific requirements.

Unlike simply registering a business name, formation creates a legally recognized entity with liability protection and the ability to enter contracts, open bank accounts, and conduct business.

## The Formation Process Step by Step

### Step 1: Choose Your State

The state where you form your LLC determines:
- Filing fees and ongoing costs
- Privacy protections
- Legal framework (Delaware has the most developed business case law)
- Tax treatment

| State | Filing Fee | Annual Fee | Best For |
|-------|-----------|------------|----------|
| Delaware | $90 | $300 | Corporate law, investor readiness |
| Wyoming | $100 | $60 | Privacy, low cost, simple requirements |
| New Mexico | $100 | $0 | Very low cost |
| California | $70 | $800 | If you have physical presence in CA |

**Important:** You do not need to form your LLC in the state where you physically operate. Many non-US founders form in Delaware or Wyoming but operate from their home country.

### Step 2: Choose a Registered Agent

Every state requires an LLC to have a registered agent with a physical address in the formation state. The registered agent receives:
- Legal summons and lawsuits
- Tax notices from state agencies
- Official government correspondence

You cannot use a P.O. Box or your personal address as a registered agent.

### Step 3: File Articles of Organization

This is the primary formation document submitted to the state. It typically requires:
- LLC name (must include LLC or Limited Liability Company)
- Registered agent name and address
- Principal office address (can be anywhere)
- Management structure (member-managed or manager-managed)
- Duration (perpetual or specific end date)

Filing fees range from $50 to $500 depending on the state.

### Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Although not required in most states, an operating agreement is essential for:
- Defining ownership percentages
- Outlining profit distribution
- Establishing management roles
- Preventing disputes between members

For single-member LLCs, an operating agreement helps demonstrate that the LLC is a separate entity from the owner.

### Step 5: Obtain an EIN

The Employer Identification Number (EIN) is required for:
- Opening US bank accounts
- Filing taxes
- Hiring employees (if applicable)

Apply via IRS Form SS-4. Non-US founders can fax the form to IRS to receive an EIN without a US phone number.

## Formation vs Registration: Understanding the Difference

| Aspect | Formation | Registration |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| What it creates | Legally recognized LLC entity | Business name within a state |
| Legal effect | Creates liability protection | No legal entity created |
| Required for | Opening bank accounts, signing contracts | Operating under a business name |
| Scope | Federal and state recognition | State-specific |

## Foreign-Owned LLC Formation Considerations

Non-US founders face unique considerations during formation:

1. **No US presence required** — You can form and operate an LLC entirely from overseas
2. **ITIN not required for formation** — EIN is the critical number, not ITIN
3. **Registered agent required** — This is the only mandatory US connection
4. **State selection matters** — Delaware and Wyoming offer the best combination of low cost and strong legal protections
