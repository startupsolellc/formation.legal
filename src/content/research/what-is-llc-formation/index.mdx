## What is an LLC?

A **Limited Liability Company (LLC)** is a business structure that combines the personal liability protection of a corporation with the tax benefits and simplicity of a partnership. LLCs are one of the most popular business entity types in the United States.

## Why Form an LLC?

There are several compelling reasons to form an LLC:

1. **Personal Asset Protection** — Your personal assets (home, savings, car) are generally protected from business debts and lawsuits.
2. **Tax Flexibility** — LLCs can choose how they're taxed: as a sole proprietorship, partnership, S-corp, or C-corp.
3. **Credibility** — Having "LLC" in your business name signals legitimacy to customers and partners.
4. **Minimal Compliance** — Compared to corporations, LLCs have fewer ongoing formalities and reporting requirements.

## Step-by-Step: How to Form an LLC

### Step 1: Choose Your State

While you can form an LLC in any state, most small businesses should form in their **home state** — the state where they primarily conduct business. Popular alternatives include:

- **Wyoming** — No state income tax, strong privacy protections
- **Delaware** — Well-established business law, Court of Chancery
- **Nevada** — No state income tax, no franchise tax

### Step 2: Choose a Name

Your LLC name must:

- Be distinguishable from other registered businesses in your state
- Include "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company"
- Not include restricted words (e.g., "Bank", "Insurance") without proper licensing

### Step 3: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every LLC must have a **registered agent** — a person or company authorized to receive legal documents on behalf of your business. The registered agent generally must maintain a street address in the formation state; this is separate from the LLC's operating, mailing, banking, or payment address.

### Step 4: File Articles of Organization

This is the official document filed with your state's Secretary of State office. Filing fees vary by state, typically ranging from **$50 to $500**.

### Step 5: Create an Operating Agreement

While not required in all states, an **Operating Agreement** is crucial. It outlines:

- Ownership percentages
- Management structure
- Profit distribution
- Member responsibilities
- Dissolution procedures

### Step 6: Obtain an EIN

An **Employer Identification Number (EIN)** from the IRS is free and necessary for:

- Opening a business bank account
- Hiring employees
- Filing business taxes

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mixing personal and business finances** — Always maintain separate bank accounts
- **Skipping the Operating Agreement** — This can lead to disputes later
- **Forgetting annual reports** — Missing deadlines can lead to dissolution
- **Choosing the wrong state** — Don't fall for "best state" marketing without considering your situation
