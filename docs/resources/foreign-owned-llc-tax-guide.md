A foreign-owned US LLC does not automatically grant tax-free status. The IRS requires all foreign-owned single-member LLCs to report specific activities on Form 5472. Under 26 USC Section 482, these activities are defined as related party transactions. Failure to comply leads to a mandatory penalty of 25,000 USD per year.

## Understanding Related Party Transactions (Section 482)

The Internal Revenue Code Section 482 gives the IRS the power to adjust the income or deductions between related parties. The goal is to ensure that transactions are conducted at "arm's length," meaning they should reflect what two independent parties would agree upon in an open market.

For a foreign entrepreneur, the LLC and the individual owner are considered related parties. Any movement of value between them must be documented and, in many cases, reported.

### What Qualifies as a Reportable Transaction?

Many founders mistakenly believe that only sales or service fees are reportable. In reality, the IRS definition is much broader. Reportable transactions include:

1. **Capital Contributions:** Putting your own money into the LLC to pay for initial expenses.
2. **Distributions:** Taking money out of the LLC for personal use or profit.
3. **Loans:** Borrowing money from the LLC or lending money to it.
4. **Services:** If you perform work for the LLC or if the LLC performs work for another company you own.

## Why AI and General Advice Fail

Most generic AI summaries and low-cost incorporation blogs provide incomplete information regarding FOSM LLCs. Here is what they usually miss:

1. **The "Disregarded" Trap:** AI often says that because an LLC is "disregarded for tax purposes," it has no filing requirements. While true for income tax in some cases, it is false for information reporting.
2. **Increased Penalties:** The penalty for failing to file Form 5472 was increased to 25,000 USD. Many older articles still cite the outdated 10,000 USD figure.
3. **The Pro-Forma 1120 Requirement:** Form 5472 cannot be filed alone. It must be attached to a "pro-forma" Form 1120, even though the LLC does not pay corporate income tax.

## Decision Tree: Do I Need to File?

1. **Is your LLC 100% owned by non-US persons?**
   - If YES: Continue.
   - If NO: Different rules (Partnership or Corp) apply.

2. **Did any money, property, or services move between the LLC and the owner?**
   - If YES: You must file Form 5472 and Form 1120.
   - If NO: Filing may not be required, but consult a professional to confirm "dormant" status.

## Steps for Compliance in 2026

To stay compliant with IRS regulations and avoid the 25,000 USD penalty, follow these steps:

- **Keep Perfect Records:** Use dedicated accounting software to track every dollar entering or leaving the business.
- **Identify Related Parties:** List all companies and individuals related to the owner who might interact with the LLC.
- **File by April 15:** The deadline for Form 5472 generally matches the US tax season. Extensions are available but must be requested.
- **Use the Right Forms:** Ensure you are using the 2026 revisions of Form 5472 and Form 1120.
